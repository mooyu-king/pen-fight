Field notes · 01

A school-desk challenge rebuilt as a quick browser duel

Pen Fight turns a familiar tabletop pastime into a compact online physics game. Instead of balancing a real pen between two fingers and snapping it across a desk, you control a red pen on an HTML5 Canvas. Press the red pen, drag in the opposite direction from the shot you want, and release it like a slingshot. The blue opponent is controlled by the computer. Both pieces slide, collide, bounce, and lose speed through friction until the active move is over. The simple objective gives every shot a clear tension: push the other pen outside the arena while keeping your own pen safely on the wood.

This version is deliberately small and immediate. There is no installation wizard, account screen, equipment shop, or long tutorial. The player above loads a self-hosted copy of the public web prototype from the same penfight.org domain. That arrangement means the surrounding page can provide reliable instructions, responsive framing, fullscreen controls, privacy information, and troubleshooting without modifying the core idea. It also keeps the game replaceable: a future edition can occupy the same local iframe path while the guide and search-friendly page remain stable.

The game was built with standard HTML, CSS, and JavaScript rather than a large engine. Its visual identity comes straight from the public project: a warm wooden desktop, graph-paper lines, and oversized red and blue pen sprites. This site borrows the structured editorial feel of an old field archive, then trades stone, parchment, and museum labels for ruled paper, ink marks, clipped notes, and a dark classroom tabletop. It is an unofficial presentation, not a claim of authorship or endorsement.

How to play · 02

Drag opposite the shot, then release.

The control behaves like a slingshot. The dotted drag line points toward your mouse, while the red pen launches in the reverse direction.

01

Start the browser game

Select the large launch button above, then choose Start Game on the original title screen. The arena opens with your red pen below the blue bot pen. If the canvas does not respond immediately, click once inside the frame so it has input focus.

02

Press and hold on the red pen

Move the pointer onto the red pen and hold the mouse button. The red pen is the player piece; the blue pen cannot be dragged because it belongs to the bot. On a touch device, press directly on the red sprite.

03

Drag away from the intended shot

If you want the red pen to travel up and right, drag the mouse down and left. The game subtracts the release position from the press position, so the launch vector is exactly opposite the drag. A longer pull produces more force until the built-in maximum is reached.

04

Release and let both pens stop

Release the button to apply the impulse. A center hit mainly pushes the blue pen forward; an off-center hit also turns both pens. Sliding and spinning gradually slow through damping. You cannot take another shot while the pieces are still moving.

05

Knock blue out without losing red

You win when enough of the blue pen crosses the arena boundary. If your red pen crosses first, the bot wins. The code checks the visible area of each rotated pen, so a pen does not need to disappear completely before the round ends.

Game physics · 03

How Pen Fight turns a mouse drag into a bouncing desk shot

Reverse-direction launch vector

The game records where the drag starts and where it ends. At release it calculates start − end for both horizontal and vertical movement. That subtraction reverses the gesture: drag left to shoot right, drag down to shoot up, or drag diagonally away from the route you want. Pull distance is multiplied to create strength, tiny drags under five internal pixels are ignored, and the final impulse is capped at 1200 so a very long pull does not create unlimited speed.

Linear speed and spin

The impulse moves the pen’s center, but the release point also matters. Force applied away from the center creates torque, which rotates the pen. This is why two pulls with similar length can produce different results. A straight, centered pull favors a predictable line; a skewed release can add rotation before contact and change which corner or side reaches the opponent first.

Rotating rectangle collisions

Both pens are treated as rotating rectangles rather than simple dots. The collision system checks their projected corners, separates overlapping bodies, finds an approximate contact point, and exchanges linear and angular velocity. Its bounce factor is 0.4, so contact returns some energy but is not perfectly elastic. A hit therefore feels like a shove with a modest rebound rather than two hard balls bouncing forever.

Friction, turns, and the edge rule

Every physics update multiplies linear speed by 0.95 and rotation by 0.98. Very small values become zero, and only then does the turn pass to the bot. For the boundary test, the game clips the rotated pen shape against the 800 × 500 canvas. When less than 60 percent of a pen remains inside—roughly 40 percent or more has crossed an edge—that pen is out. Blue out means you win; red out means the bot wins.

Tactics notebook · 04

Pen Fight strategy: better angles, safer power, and shorter knockout routes

The blue targetDo not merely hit it—push its center toward the nearest edge while steering your red pen toward safer open wood.

Step one: identify the nearest edge

Do not aim at the blue pen’s center automatically. Compare its distance to the top, bottom, left, and right boundaries, then choose the shortest knockout route. If blue is nearest the top edge, your red pen should hit upward—which means pulling the mouse downward because the launch reverses the drag. The goal is not the biggest impact; it is the least force needed to cross the nearest line.

Step two: use center hits to push and side hits to turn

Contact near the blue pen’s center transfers linear motion more directly and is ideal when blue already points toward an edge. Contact near a side or end adds more rotation, which can turn an awkward horizontal target toward the boundary but is harder to predict. Practice center hits first. Use off-center contact when blue has an unfavorable angle or when a straight hit would carry red along the same dangerous route.

Step three: control power instead of always pulling fully

Force increases with drag distance, but more is not always better. Red keeps moving after contact, so an excessive direct hit may eject blue and still rebound red across another boundary. Use a short or medium pull when blue is already near an edge. Reserve longer pulls for large gaps where red also has plenty of open desk behind its expected rebound.

Step four: plan red’s second path

Every shot contains two routes: the attack before contact and the rebound afterward. If red is near the left boundary, avoid a contact angle that sends it farther left. The ideal collision pushes blue along its shortest route out while redirecting red toward the middle. Even without an immediate knockout, finishing near the center reduces the bot’s chance of winning with one short return shot.

Step five: exploit the 40 percent boundary rule

A pen does not have to disappear completely. Once roughly two-fifths of its rotated area crosses the canvas edge, the round ends. A light finishing push is therefore safer when part of blue already hangs over a boundary. The same rule is dangerous for red: a long rotated tip can increase the outside area quickly, so avoid a high-power chase when your own pen is already straddling the line.

A practical training routine

Begin with ten purely horizontal or vertical shots to build reverse-drag muscle memory. Next, use one angle with short, medium, and long pulls to compare distance. Finally, practice hitting blue at the center, one-quarter point, and end. Changing only one variable per attempt teaches speed, rotation, and rebound distance faster than random maximum-power flicks.

Rules & rhythm · 05

Win by forcing blue out; lose when red crosses first

Traditional pen fighting varies from classroom to classroom. This browser interpretation chooses one clear rule set: you control red, the computer controls blue, turns alternate after motion stops, and the arena boundary decides the result. A round can end through a successful attack or through your own overpowered shot. That makes self-preservation part of every offensive move.

The scoreboard increases the relevant side after an out-of-bounds result, while Restart creates a fresh pair of pens. The current build is a short arcade prototype rather than a campaign: it has no online matchmaking, permanent ranking, equipment progression, or cloud save. Its replay value comes from learning how pullback direction, drag length, contact point, spin, and remaining desk space combine.

What the bot changes

The local bot supplies an immediate reply once your red pen has fully stopped. It does not need a multiplayer server or another player. Its move means the safest end position is usually near the middle, where you have room on several sides. Parking near an edge gives the bot a short winning route even if your previous attack moved blue into danger.

Because the collision has only partial bounce and both pens lose speed continuously, careful medium shots are usually easier to repeat than dramatic maximum-power hits. The most reliable strategy is incremental: move blue toward its nearest edge, keep red inside a broad central zone, then use a smaller finishing push as soon as blue is partly over the line.

Browser desk · 05

Desktop, mobile, fullscreen, and troubleshooting

Desktop is the recommended setup

Use a current version of Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari with JavaScript enabled. A mouse offers the clearest drag-and-release gesture. Keep browser zoom near 100 percent if the pointer and pen appear misaligned, and avoid beginning a drag outside the canvas. Fullscreen can create more visual room, but the internal game still uses its original 800 by 500 coordinate system.

Mobile play is experimental

The public project README explicitly identifies responsiveness as unfinished and touch input as experimental. This site scales the canvas visually so it fits narrower screens, but visual scaling cannot guarantee that every device maps touch coordinates perfectly. Landscape orientation gives the arena more width. If a finger press lands away from the visible pen, return to normal browser zoom or use a desktop computer rather than repeatedly fighting the offset.

If the Start Game link fails

The hosted copy uses an explicit play/index.html link instead of relying on directory index behavior. If navigation still stalls, reload the surrounding page, launch again, and check whether a privacy extension has blocked local scripts. The game does not need third-party cookies, but it does need its JavaScript modules and local image files.

If the canvas is blank

Wait a moment, then reload once. The package is small, so a persistent blank area usually points to blocked JavaScript, a failed asset request, or an unusually old browser. Opening the player in fullscreen will not repair a missing script. Update the browser, temporarily disable aggressive script blocking for penfight.org, and try again.

If dragging does nothing

Click directly on the red pen, hold, move, and release. The blue pen belongs to the bot and is not your draggable piece. Also confirm that no game-over or menu overlay is covering the canvas. Resume from the menu or choose Restart to restore an active round.

Privacy and local hosting

The core game is served from this same domain rather than framed from a third-party deployment. That reduces dependency on an external live copy, but the website host and lightweight analytics service may still receive standard technical information. Read the Privacy Policy for details. The source repository currently has no clearly identified license file, so public commercial use should be confirmed with its creators.

Quick answers · 06

Pen Fight FAQ

Controls, compatibility, credits, and common browser questions.

01Can I play Pen Fight online for free?+

Yes. The browser game on this page can be played without a download, account, or payment. Press Start the fight, then use the game’s own Start Game button to enter the arena.

02How do I flick the red pen?+

Press on the red pen, drag in the opposite direction from the shot you want, then release. Drag down to shoot up, left to shoot right, or diagonally away from your intended route. A longer pull creates more force until the built-in cap is reached.

03What is the goal of the Pen Fight game?+

Keep your red pen on the desk while forcing the blue bot pen beyond the arena boundary. The round ends when roughly 40 percent or more of either rotated pen crosses an edge: blue out means you win, while red out means the bot wins.

04Does Pen Fight work on phones and tablets?+

The source includes experimental touch input, but its authors describe the current build as desktop-optimized. A modern phone may run it, yet dragging can be less reliable than using a mouse or trackpad.

05Why does the game open on a second title screen?+

The first cover belongs to penfight.org and prevents the game from loading before you want it. The second title screen is part of the original game package. Select Start Game there to reach the canvas.

06Can I play Pen Fight in fullscreen?+

Yes. Start the game and use the Fullscreen control beneath the player. Your browser may ask for permission, and you can usually leave fullscreen with Escape.

07Is penfight.org the official developer website?+

No. This is an unofficial browser portal. The embedded prototype was created by the contributors credited in its public repository; this surrounding guide is independently written.

08Does the game save progress?+

The current prototype is a short round-based game rather than a campaign. The visible score lasts during the active session, but you should not expect long-term profiles, unlocks, or cloud saves.

09What should I try if the canvas is blank or controls do not respond?+

Reload the page, use a current browser, allow JavaScript, and click inside the frame before dragging. On mobile, rotate to landscape. If input remains offset, switch to a desktop browser at normal zoom.
